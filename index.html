01 / EXPERIMENT

Let's test what you think

What is experimental probability?

Experimental probability is an estimate of how likely something is to happen, based on the results of an experiment. We find it by repeating the experiment, counting how often an event happens, and comparing that count to the total number of trials.

Experimental probability = number of times the event happens ÷ total number of trials

For example, if the Two of Clubs follows the first ace in 3 out of 100 shuffled decks, its experimental probability is 3 ÷ 100 = 3%. That is an estimate based on those 100 trials.

03 / THE REVEAL

Why is it a tie?

The two cards are eliminated in different ways, but there's a surprisingly simple argument that works for either one.

51
Take out the target card.

Remove the A♠ or the 2♣, then shuffle the other 51 cards. Find their first ace.

52
Choose an insertion gap.

There are 52 equally likely places to put the removed card back: before, between, or after the 51 cards.

1
Only one gap wins.

Exactly one gap puts that card immediately after the first ace, without changing which ace comes first.

THE MATHEMATICAL RESULT
A♠ = 2♣ = 1 / 52

Each card has exactly a 1.923% chance of appearing immediately after the first ace. Different stories, identical odds.
